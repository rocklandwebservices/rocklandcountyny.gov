SUMMARY OF PROPOSED CHANGES TO THE CHARTER

 

  1. General clean-up provisions throughout the charter
    1. Updating certain sections by renumbering or reformatting to make it more readable.
    2. Rewording, including increased use of specific titles of officers, to avoid ambiguity and bring the language into context with the new formatting, while keeping usage the same.

RATIONALE – removes ambiguity and enhances readability without changing substance

  1. Amendments to conform with longstanding practices and add clarification.
    1. Updating definitions of “Board”, “Department,” “Executive Branch,” and “Legislative Branch,” “Unit,” and “Unit of appropriation,”
    2. Amending the powers of the County Legislature to clearly state that it may make decisions on appeals as provided for by local law – a power it already possesses and exercises.

RATIONALE – makes language conform to actual practice.

  1. Changes to Article II – The County Legislature
    1. Amending the types of resolutions that constitute internal Legislative business not subject to County Executive veto
      1. Used to be limited to establishing the rules and regulations of the Legislature, the setting of a date for a public hearing, other matters pertaining solely to the conduct of its own procedures including the election of a Chairperson and Vice-Chairperson, the confirmation or rejection of County Executive appointments, and appointments vested solely in the Legislature by this Charter of local law.
      2. Now adds resolutions including the following: legislative grants pursuant to state or federal authority, contracts with vendors for professional service solely on behalf of the Legislature, non-binding memorializing resolutions, and resolutions authorizing litigation on behalf of the Legislature.

RATIONALE – enhances separation of powers and ensures independence of the Legislature with respect to matters that fall within the Legislature’s sphere.

  1. Authorizing and making official the existing discretionary practice of appointing a ‘Second Vice-Chairperson’ for the County Legislature

RATIONALE – ensures clear line of succession

  1. Amending the powers of the Legislature to authorize it to appoint its own Legislative Counsel and specifying the powers and duties

RATIONALE – codifies and formalizes existing longstanding practice

  1. Amending the procedure for Legislative confirmation of County Executive appointments
    1. Previously, the Legislature’s failure to confirm/reject an appointment within 60 days meant the appointment was deemed confirmed. Now it is deemed rejected.

RATIONALE – resolves existing conflict, where one section said appointments were deemed confirmed and another said they were deemed rejected

  1. New provision added – if the same person has been presented and rejected twice for the same office, the person may not be resubmitted for the remainder of the County Executive’s term.

RATIONALE – reduces bureaucracy by preventing repeated resolutions

  1. Amending the procedure for approval and execution of contracts over $100,000 – removed section authorizing Legislature to approve all contracts over $100,000 and created an entirely new section in Article IV – County Budget (see rationale below in Section V Subsection C)
  1. Changes to Article III – Executive Branch
    1. Amending the power of the County Executive to authorize corrections to erroneously assessed property taxes – increased threshold amount from $1,000 to $5,000

RATIONALE – reduces number of resolutions needed due to a monetary threshold that is too low, thereby enhancing governmental efficiency

  1. Amending the powers of the County Executive to include acting as the Tourism Promotion Agency for the County

RATIONALE – required for state funding

  1. Amending the procedure for approval and execution of contracts over $100,000 – removed section authorizing County Executive to execute all contracts under $100,000 and created an entirely new section in Article IV – County Budget (see rationale below in Section V Subsection C)
  2. Amending the process of confirming Department heads and board members who serve at the County Executive’s discretion, and the manner vacancies and holdovers are handled
    1. Previously, stated only that the County Executive appoints all department heads subject to legislative confirmation. Now, clarifies the long-term practice that appointment power includes all members of county boards and commissions, and the heads of departments and divisions as shall be listed in separate legislation.
    2. Clarifies that where vacancies occur other than by expiration of term, they shall be filled by appointment for the balance of the unexpired term by the County Executive, subject to Legislative confirmation.

RATIONALE – removes ambiguity as to length of terms for appointments filling vacancies and which positions require legislative approval

  1. Changes to Article IV – County Budget
    1. Amending the budget process by no longer requiring formal departmental proposals
    2. Amending the deadline and composition of the Capital Projects Committee to bring it in line with long-standing practices
      1. Adds the chair of the Planning and Public Works Committee, the Commissioner of Finance and the Commissioner of General Services, while removing the Commissioner of Planning
      2. Changes the deadline for the annual report from June 1st to September 15th

RATIONALE – Amends existing language to formalize existing practices

  1. Creating a new section entitled Execution of Contracts which changes the threshold amounts of contracts requiring full legislative approval
    1. Contracts up to $100,000 may be executed solely at the County Executive’s discretion
    2. Contracts in excess of $250,000 require the approval of the County Legislature
    3. Contracts between $100,000-$250,000 require either the unanimous approval of the newly created Contracts Committee (Chair of the Legislature, Minority Leader, and chair of the Budget and Finance Committee) or the approval of the Legislature

RATIONALE – Reduces bureaucracy and increases efficiency by speeding up the process while maintaining legislative oversight as the appropriating body. Number hasn’t been raised in 30+ years, $100k in 1996 is worth over $210k today, and neighboring counties have higher thresholds.

  1. Changes to Article XIX – Commission on Human Rights
    1. Renaming the “Commission on Human Rights” to the “Department of Human Rights”
    2. Clarifying that the Commissioner of Human Rights is the administrative head of the Department of Human Rights
    3. Expressly providing for thirteen members of the Commission on Human Rights who serve for staggered terms of five (5) years

RATIONALE – creates consistency by naming the Human Rights department like other similar departments, and ensures that structure has the power of the Charter behind it, not a simple resolution

  1. Miscellaneous Changes to Various Departments
    1. Amending the powers of the Commissioner of Planning to conform with the amendment to the Capital Projects Committee – REMOVES CONTRADICTION
    2. Abolishing the Department of Hospitals and the title of Commissioner of Hospitals, since there no longer is a hospital – REFLECTS REALITY
    3. Authorizing the Sheriff to appoint additional Undersheriffs as authorized by the budget – ALLOWS FOR FLEXIBILITY
    4. Requiring the Public Defender and County Attorney to be admitted to the bar for at least ten years as a qualification of office – ENSURES APPROPRIATE LEVEL OF EXPERTISE
